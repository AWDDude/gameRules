# Rummy 500 Rules

## Overview

Rummy 500 (also known as 500 Rum or Pinochle Rummy) is a popular variant of rummy where players earn points by melding cards and lose points for cards left in their hand. The first player to reach 500 points wins the game.

## Players

2-8 players (best with 3-5 players)

## Equipment

- Standard 52-card deck
- For 5+ players, use two decks shuffled together
- Pen and paper for scorekeeping

## Card Values

- **Ace**: 15 points (can be high or low in runs)
- **Face cards (K, Q, J)**: 10 points each
- **Number cards (10-2)**: 5 points each
- **Jokers** (if used): 20 points if left in hand, in a meld it is the value of the card it represents

## Objective

Be the first player to reach 500 points by forming melds (sets and runs) and laying them off on the table.

## Setup

1. Choose a dealer (rotate clockwise each hand)
2. Dealer shuffles and deals:
   - **2 players**: 13 cards each
   - **3-4 players**: 7 cards each
   - **5-8 players**: 6 cards each
3. Place remaining cards face-down as the stock pile
4. Turn the top card face-up to start the discard pile
5. Spread the discard pile so all cards are visible

## Gameplay

### Turn Sequence

On your turn:

1. **Draw**: Either draw from the stock pile OR take one or more cards from the discard pile
2. **Steal Jokers** (optional): Replace a Joker in any meld with the actual card it represents
2. **Meld** (optional): Lay down sets or runs, or add cards to existing melds
3. **Discard**: Place one card face-up on the discard pile

### Drawing from the Discard Pile

The discard pile is the key feature that distinguishes Rummy 500:

- Cards in the discard pile are **spread out** so all cards are visible
- You may take **any card** from the discard pile (not just the top card)
- When you take a card, you **must also take all cards above it**
- You **must immediately use** the card you targeted in a meld during that turn
- Cards taken along with your target card can be used freely or saved in your hand

**Example**: If the discard pile shows 5♠, 7♦, 9♣, K♥ (top), and you want the 7♦, you must take 7♦, 9♣, and K♥ (all three cards). You must use the 7♦ in a meld immediately.

### Melds

There are two types of melds:

#### Sets (Groups)
- Three or four cards of the **same rank** but different suits
- Example: 7♠ 7♥ 7♦ or K♣ K♦ K♥ K♠

#### Runs (Sequences)
- Three or more **consecutive cards** of the **same suit**
- Example: 4♥ 5♥ 6♥ 7♥ or 10♠ J♠ Q♠ K♠ A♠
- Ace can be high (A-K-Q) or low (A-2-3) but **not both** (K-A-2 is invalid)

### Using Jokers (Wild Cards)

When playing with Jokers, add 2 Jokers to the deck. Jokers are wild cards that can substitute for any card:

#### In Sets
- A Joker can represent any rank
- Example: 8♠ 8♥ Joker (Joker acts as any 8)
- Maximum one Joker per set of three, or two Jokers in a set of four

#### In Runs
- A Joker can fill any position in a sequence
- Example: 5♦ Joker 7♦ (Joker acts as 6♦)
- Example: Joker 9♣ 10♣ J♣ (Joker acts as 8♣)
- You can use multiple Jokers in a run: 4♠ Joker Joker 7♠ (represents 4♠ 5♠ 6♠ 7♠)

#### Stealing Jokers
- On your turn, replace a Joker in any meld with the actual card it represents
- Take the Joker into your hand, **must immediately use** in a meld or discard it
- Example: If a meld shows 5♥ Joker 7♥, you can replace the Joker with 6♥ and take the Joker

#### Scoring Jokers
- **In melds**: A Joker scores the value of the card it represents
  - Joker as K♥ = 10 points
  - Joker as A♠ = 15 points
  - Joker as 7♦ = 5 points
- **In your hand**: Joker left unmelded = 20 points penalty

#### Strategy with Jokers
- Jokers are powerful but risky—worth 15 points if stuck in your hand
- Use Jokers to complete high-value melds (runs with face cards or Aces)
- Early in the game, save Jokers for crucial melds
- Late in the game, meld Jokers quickly to avoid the 15-point penalty
- Don't hoard multiple Jokers—the penalty can be devastating

### Laying Off

After you've made your initial meld, you can **lay off** cards by adding them to any meld on the table (yours or opponents'):

- Add a fourth card to a set of three
- Extend a run on either end
- Cards laid off are placed in front of **you**, not the original meld owner
- You score points for cards you lay off, even on opponent melds

**Example**: If an opponent has 5♣ 6♣ 7♣ on the table, you can lay off 4♣ or 8♣ (or both) in front of yourself.

### Discarding

- You **must** discard one card to end your turn, unless the player is going out
- Place it face-up on top of the discard pile
- You cannot discard a card you just drew from the discard pile on the same turn

## Going Out

A player "goes out" when they meld or lay off all their cards (with or without a final discard). This ends the hand immediately.

You do **not** need to discard your last card—you can go out by melding all remaining cards.

## Scoring

After someone goes out:

### Points Earned
- Add up the point value of **all cards you melded** or laid off

### Points Lost
- Subtract the point value of **all cards remaining in your hand**

**Example Scoring**:
- You melded: K♥ K♠ K♦ (30 points) and 4♣ 5♣ 6♣ (15 points) = 45 points
- You laid off: 7♣ on an opponent's run (7 points) = 7 points
- Cards in hand: 9♦ A♠ (9 + 15 = 24 points)
- **Your score this hand**: 45 + 7 - 24 = **28 points**

## Winning

- Keep a running total of each player's score across multiple hands
- The **first player to reach 500 points** (or more) wins the game

## Strategy Tips

1. **Watch the discard pile carefully**: Plan ahead for cards you want to take
2. **Balance melds and deadwood**: Don't hold too many high cards hoping to meld them
3. **Lay off when possible**: Get points on the board to minimize risk
4. **Track opponent melds**: Know what cards might be useful to them
5. **Time your going out**: Sometimes it's worth holding back if opponents have many cards
6. **Manage Aces carefully**: They're worth 15 points—meld or discard them quickly
7. **Use the discard pile aggressively**: Taking multiple cards can give you big melding opportunities
8. **Joker strategy**: Use Jokers for high-value melds, but don't hold them too long—the 15-point penalty hurts

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Forgetting to use the targeted discard card immediately in a meld
- Taking too many high-value cards without melding them quickly
- Not spreading the discard pile properly
- Confusing scoring (remember: melds are positive, hand cards are negative)
- Laying off cards in front of the original meld owner instead of yourself

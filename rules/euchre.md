# Euchre Rules

## Overview

Euchre is a trick-taking card game typically played with 4 players in partnerships of 2. The game uses a deck of 24 cards (9, 10, J, Q, K, A of each suit).

## The Deck

- 24 cards total: 9, 10, Jack, Queen, King, Ace in all four suits
- Cards rank from high to low (in non-trump suits): A, K, Q, J, 10, 9
- Trump suit ranking is special (see Trump Ranking below)

## Trump Ranking

When a suit is declared trump, the card rankings change:

1. **Right Bower** - Jack of trump suit (highest card)
2. **Left Bower** - Jack of same color as trump (second highest)
3. **Ace of trump**
4. **King of trump**
5. **Queen of trump**
6. **10 of trump**
7. **9 of trump**

**Important**: The Left Bower is considered part of the trump suit, not its original suit.

Example: If Hearts is trump:
- Jack of Hearts (Right Bower) is the highest card
- Jack of Diamonds (Left Bower) is the second highest card and counts as a Heart
- The Diamond suit only has 5 cards (no Jack)

## Setup

- 4 players sit across from each other, forming 2 partnerships
- Partners sit opposite each other (North-South vs East-West)
- Deal rotates clockwise
- Dealer deals 5 cards to each player in batches of 2-3 or 3-2
- One card is turned face-up on the table (the "upcard")
- Remaining 3 cards form the "kitty"

## Bidding (Ordering Up)

### Round 1: The Upcard

Starting with the player to the dealer's left and proceeding clockwise:
- Players may "order it up" (make the upcard's suit trump)
- Players may pass

If any player orders it up:
- The dealer picks up the upcard and discards any card face-down
- The upcard's suit becomes trump
- The team that ordered up becomes the "makers"
- Play begins

### Round 2: Choosing Another Suit

If all players pass in Round 1:
- The upcard is turned face-down
- Starting again with the player to dealer's left, players may:
  - Name any suit EXCEPT the upcard's suit as trump
  - Pass

If all players pass in Round 2:
- The hand is thrown in and cards are redealt by the next dealer

### Going Alone

When ordering up or naming trump, a player may declare "alone":
- Their partner sits out the hand
- The declaring player plays against both opponents
- Potential for higher scoring (see Scoring below)

### Stick the Dealer

In some variations, the dealer cannot pass in Round 2 and must name a suit if everyone else passes.

## Playing the Hand

### Leading

- Player to dealer's left leads first (unless someone is going alone, then it's the player to their left)
- Players must follow suit if able
- If unable to follow suit, any card may be played
- Highest trump wins the trick; if no trump, highest card of led suit wins
- Winner of trick leads next

### Remember

- Left Bower is part of the trump suit
- If trump is led, you must play trump if you have it (including Left Bower)
- If the Left Bower's original suit is led, you do NOT have to play the Left Bower (it's not that suit anymore)

## Scoring

### Normal Play (No Alone)

**Makers (team that called trump) take:**
- 3 or 4 tricks = 1 point
- All 5 tricks (march) = 2 points

**Makers take fewer than 3 tricks:**
- Defenders score 2 points (this is called being "euchred")

### Going Alone

**Lone player takes:**
- All 5 tricks = 4 points
- 3 or 4 tricks = 1 point

**Lone player takes fewer than 3 tricks:**
- Defenders score 2 points

### Winning the Game

- First team to 10 points wins (some variations play to 5, 7, or 15)
- Must win by at least 2 points in some variations

## Strategy Tips

### Bidding

- Generally need at least 3 trump cards to order up safely
- Having a Bower (especially Right) makes ordering up much stronger
- Position matters: ordering from 1st or 2nd seat is riskier than 3rd or 4th
- Consider your partner's likely holdings

### Trump Selection

- **Next**: The same color as the rejected upcard (common strategic choice)
- **Green**: A suit of different color (riskier)
- Having an Ace and a Bower in the same suit is very strong for calling trump

### Play

- Lead trump to draw out opponents' trump early if your team is strong
- Lead your strongest suit if you don't have trump advantage
- Remember what has been played (especially Bowers and Aces)
- Second player should generally play low if partner is winning
- Third player should play high to win or create uncertainty

### Going Alone

Consider going alone when you have:
- 3+ trump including a Bower
- Right Bower with 2-3 other trump
- Strong side Aces for non-trump tricks

## Common Variations

### Stick the Dealer
Dealer must call trump in Round 2 if all others pass.

### Canadian Loner
Defending team may also call a lone hand after an opponent orders up.

### No Ace, No Face, No Trump
If dealt a hand with no Aces, face cards, or trump (from upcard), player may declare and redeal.

### British Euchre
Uses 25 cards (adds 8s) and different scoring variations.

### Railroad Euchre
- Joker added as highest trump
- Defending team can call lone hand
- Various scoring bonuses

## Terminology

- **Bower**: Jack (Right Bower = Jack of trump, Left Bower = Jack of same color)
- **Maker**: Team that called trump
- **Defender**: Team that did not call trump
- **March**: Taking all 5 tricks
- **Euchred**: Makers fail to take 3 tricks
- **Next**: Same color as rejected upcard
- **Green**: Different color from rejected upcard
- **Kitty**: The 3 cards not dealt to any player
- **Upcard**: The face-up card from the kitty used in Round 1 bidding
- **Lay down**: When partner of lone player puts cards face-down

## Example Hand Walkthrough

### Setup
- North and South are partners vs East and West
- West is dealer
- Cards dealt, upcard is 10 of Spades

### Round 1 Bidding
- North (left of dealer): Pass
- East: Pass
- South: Pass
- West: Pass
- Upcard turned down

### Round 2 Bidding
- North: "Hearts" (calls trump)
- North and South are now the "makers"

### Trump Hierarchy (Hearts is trump)
1. Jack of Hearts (Right Bower)
2. Jack of Diamonds (Left Bower) - now counts as a Heart!
3. Ace of Hearts
4. King of Hearts
5. Queen of Hearts
6. 10 of Hearts
7. 9 of Hearts

### Play
- North leads: 9 of Clubs
- East plays: Queen of Clubs (highest so far)
- South plays: Ace of Clubs (takes the trick)
- West plays: 10 of Clubs

South wins trick and leads next.

### Result
- If North-South take 3+ tricks: they score 1 point (2 if they take all 5)
- If they take fewer than 3: East-West score 2 points (makers got euchred)
